# Snow Bubble

A fully formed soap bubble drifts in from a seeded direction above a moonlit snowfield. Its wind-influenced path eases into the snow, its approaching shadow tightens beneath it, and cooling starts only after touchdown. Ice crystals nucleate near the lower freeze front, move over the curved liquid film, grow and interlock. The crystals live on the surface, not in the air inside the bubble.

Every bubble starts with continuous seeded conditions for temperature, film thickness, drainage, permeability, circulation and sensitivity to stress. These conditions evolve with the seeded wind and slow thermal drift. No outcome category or failure time is assigned at birth. Every bubble eventually ruptures or collapses before another forms. The remaining liquid can rupture during partial freezing, the upper dome can deflate above a frozen base, or ice can break into irregular sheets. Complete freezing is a temporary material state, never an ending. Weather continues loading the frozen shell, accumulated stress raises its fracture rate, and its seeded failure budget continues running. A stalled front can advance again when conditions change. There is no fixed lifetime or scheduled sequence of failures.

Crystal arrivals use a continuous seeded random process with no maximum count. Their rate responds to surface area, cooling and remaining liquid. Snowfall and shooting stars have no concurrency ceiling. Droplet, ice-sheet, star-field and frost-detail counts are sampled from seeded distributions with no fixed count or upper cutoff. Objects disappear when their lifetime or physical scene ends. Spatial lookup speeds up crystal contact checks without discarding crystals or limiting their number.

The freezing and partial-collapse sequences are inspired by [How soap bubbles freeze](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-10021-6). A shared spatial current carries nearby crystals together. Local heat released by growing crystals disturbs that current. Warm film rises across the front and returns down the back, carrying detached crystals around the shell with smoothly changing velocity. Mobile crystals can pass one another without becoming permanently stuck. Contact with anchored ice and advancing ice coverage gradually reduce mobility until patches interlock. The flow is projected onto the curved liquid film and slows as ice closes over it. Crystal habit varies continuously between translucent faceted plates and branched forms. Main arms and side branches grow outward along the curved shell, with seeded asymmetry. Translucent patches and surface bridges join crystals where their growing branches touch. The freeze boundary follows an uneven seeded front. Cracks spread from a seeded tear and release ice patches at independent times. Patches also release when broken neighbors leave them without a connection to the supporting base. Each patch carries its crystals, folds inward, falls and turns without being uniformly squashed, and contacts the snow along its lowest rotated edge. Snow absorbs the impact, sliding and rotation lose energy, and the pieces settle before their remnants fade. A shell with no separating cracks buckles as one piece. Crystals remain attached to the ice during failure, without a cloud of snowflakes released from the enclosed air. The evolving conditions, rates, rendering and fracture are qualitative generative models rather than quantitative predictions of a real bubble.

Open `/snow-bubble/` with the local server running, or `/snow-bubble/embed/` for the scene alone. The shared overlay supplies the clock, seed log, author credit, share and fullscreen controls. Press L for the log, H to hide the overlay and F for fullscreen. Text uses Pixelspace. Counters track bubbles, ice crystals, liquid ruptures, frozen shells and collapses.

The sky uses One Sock's star palette, shimmer and stylized Orion, Cassiopeia and Ursa Major's Big Dipper patterns, with seeded field-star density. It is not a location-specific astronomical chart. Shooting stars cross above cached mountain ridges and snowy banks.

One shared clock-seeded generator drives every random choice. Independent exponential event budgets control crystal arrivals, wind changes, snowfall, shooting stars, rupture, deflation, fracture and pauses. Unit vectors keep crystals on the spherical shell. Each branch point is mapped onto that sphere before projection, with front and back segments separated at the silhouette. Crystal brightness follows surface angle and interlocking growth. Cached branch geometry and terrain keep rendering light. Drawing, resizing and quality settings consume no random values and do not alter the seeded world.

The scene uses smooth procedural Canvas 2D at device resolution. A close, low view frames the bubble against softly focused hills and detailed foreground snow. After landing, its base stays planted in a shallow indentation. The camera accommodates the sampled radius rather than limiting the bubble's size. Nearby snow grains overlap the film, while ice grows from the contact line. Uneven film bands, landscape reflections and faint curved moonlight describe the thin shell without magnifying the enclosed air like glass. Film drainage drives the changing interference bands. Air loss shrinks the bubble while its contact plane stays planted. Wind, surface currents and snowfall ease toward their changing conditions. Shell flex follows seeded noise, and camera framing eases between bubbles while the landscape stays fixed. These responses reuse the existing seeded formation, weather and drainage parameters. Reduced motion selects a still from naturally developed seeded crystal growth. Embeds follow the shared quality, pause/play and recording behavior without an overlay.

With `?debug`, `window.SB` exposes `pause()`, `run()`, `step(seconds)`, `draw()`, `state()` and `fire(event)`. State returns a copy. Events are `form`, `nucleate`, `current`, `snow`, `meteor`, `burst`, `collapse` and `fracture`, with physically invalid events rejected. Debug events exercise the same processes as the running scene. They do not assign a future outcome. `?speed=8` accelerates playback. Debug pages also expose `render_game_to_text()` and `advanceTime(ms)`.

Run `node snow-bubble/check-crystals.js` for curved branch geometry, silhouette and pole projection, progressive side-branch growth, uneven frost, independent ice release, ground collision, settled debris and render-state isolation. The data file contains metadata and counters. Templates use the shared layouts, and scene code, styles, logo and share preview live in `assets/`.
